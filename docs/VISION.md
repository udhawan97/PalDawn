# PalDawn mission and future vision

## Mission

Help people understand what happens inside the body by turning sourced causal
explanations into explorable, time-based visual journeys.

PalDawn should let a learner start with the whole person, reveal the relevant
systems, follow a condition or substance step by step, and move from body to
system to organ to structure to a carefully bounded microscopic view. The same
visual beat should support two reading depths:

- **Plain English** — technical but understandable to a curious 15-year-old.
- **Clinical terms** — deeper mechanisms, terminology, uncertainty, and direct
  evidence links for advanced learners and professionals.

## North star

Build a professional-grade body learning and research companion where someone
can search a structure, disease, symptom mechanism, or major medicine and see a
source-linked simulation of how it interacts with the body over time.

The long-range experience includes:

1. **Whole-person entry.** Begin with a detailed human form, then reveal only
   the systems relevant to the selected topic.
2. **Causal playback.** Play, pause, scrub, and compare the ordered mechanism:
   what changes first, what responds next, and how downstream effects emerge.
3. **Semantic zoom.** Move deliberately from body → system → organ → structure
   → cellular or molecular model without pretending one continuous mesh can
   carry every scale accurately.
4. **Disease journeys.** Explain representative mechanisms, variation,
   symptoms, complications, and uncertainty with claim-level evidence.
5. **Drug journeys.** Trace administration, absorption, distribution, target
   interaction, metabolism, elimination, common effects, and major safety
   boundaries for familiar and clinically important medicines.
6. **Research mode.** Expose source metadata, model assumptions, versioned
   evidence, confidence, and reviewer status so professionals can inspect the
   basis of a visualization rather than accepting it as a black box.

## What “simulation” must mean

PalDawn must distinguish authored visual explanation from validated numerical
simulation. A cinematic sequence is not automatically a physiological model.
Every future topic declares which level it provides:

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Conceptual map | Authored relationships and sequence; illustrative geometry |
| Evidence-linked mechanism | Claims map to sources; assumptions and uncertainty are visible |
| Quantitative model | Equations, parameters, units, validation data, and limits are documented |
| Clinically reviewed module | A named qualified reviewer approves a recorded claim and visual scope |

No level may imply diagnosis, individual treatment advice, or patient-specific
prediction unless a future regulated product scope, evidence program, privacy
model, and authorization explicitly support that use.

## Quality bar

- Anatomical assets require per-object source, creator, license, lineage, and
  named qualified-human review before authoritative use.
- Medical and pharmacology claims require claim-level sources, visible dates,
  uncertainty, and named qualified-human review before publication as reviewed
  content.
- Visual detail must improve shape, material, lighting, depth, motion, and
  interaction—not merely add labels or increase polygon count.
- Plain and Clinical readings share one mechanism timeline so the science does
  not change when explanation depth changes.
- Accessibility includes reduced motion, text equivalents, keyboard control,
  captions, contrast, and a non-WebGL route.
- Patient or personal health data is out of scope for the current static app.

## Current boundary

The current release is an educational preview with project-authored procedural
geometry. It demonstrates the interaction model and source-linked disease
journeys, but it is not reviewed anatomy, a pharmacology simulator, a validated
physiology model, a diagnostic device, or a professional clinical reference.
The path from today's prototype to the north star is gated by evidence,
provenance, validation, and qualified review—not visual polish alone.
