# PalDawn v0.4.0 — Study and Reliability

This release consolidates the verified mainline changes since v0.3.0.

## In the public app

- Atlas Wayfinder connects named structures to existing disease steps. Research
  Lens keeps the current step's sources accessible while studying.
- The curriculum browser organizes 50 condition entries: ten existing previews
  and forty planned entries. The hypertension proposal remains gated; neither
  the catalog nor the proposal represents fifty published medical lessons.
- The First Light workspace searches authored tracks and private notes, opens
  matching comparisons without moving the journey, and rejects backups larger
  than 256 KiB before reading them.
- Local-data recovery, cross-tab reset/import transactions and PWA update
  handoffs better protect unsaved work. Keyboard focus, narrow-screen layouts
  and the living-mark interface have been refined.

## Included as development candidates

Source includes the credited male/female Anatomy Lab viewer, redesigned site
candidate, and isolated heart/vessel-flow workbenches. **They are excluded from
the normal public build.** Anatomy packs are prepared separately and are not
included in the source archives. The female assembly has partial skeleton and
muscle coverage; anatomy and ailment coverage are incomplete. Qualified anatomy
and clinical review remain pending. Animation and cutaway tools are visual study
controls, not validated physiology or pathology simulations.

## Verification and distribution

Publication requires CI and Pages to succeed on the exact tagged mainline
commit. CI covers type checking, the public build and deterministic contracts,
Chromium/WebKit browser tests, graphics checks, provenance and dependency-license
inventory. Source ZIP and tar archives are supplied by GitHub; this release has
no native-app installer or bundled reference-model download.

The public model remains conceptual and not to scale. Source-linked educational
content is unreviewed synthesis, not diagnosis or treatment guidance. Automated
checks and citations do not establish medical accuracy.
